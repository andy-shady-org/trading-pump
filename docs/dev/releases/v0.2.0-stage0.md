### Analysis
1. **The reported profit is a pricing artefact (BUG-104).** 137 positions (12%) showed at least 1.5× within 10 s of entry; together they account for +64.46 SOL, more than the whole reported gross. The top two "returned" 34.4 SOL and 17.0 SOL on 0.01 SOL. Reading their bonding curves on-chain showed why:
   - Paper entries are priced from the stream's create event (or the 27.96 SOL initial-curve default). The first health-loop refresh then reads the real curve account and prices the position from it.
   - The outlier curves are a different variant: the account has extra non-zero data after byte 81, `virtual_sol_reserves` is between 764 and 105,860 SOL while `real_sol_reserves` is 0 and `real_token_reserves` is untouched, and the curve has only its creation signature (nobody ever traded it). The decoder treats the field as SOL, so the market cap jumps 25×–3,500× in one refresh and tier 1, tier 2 and the trailing stop all fire at prices no one could sell at.
   - 263 of the 1,159 entries had mints not ending in `pump`; 39 of them produced +58.9 SOL of the artefact profit.
2. **Without the artefacts the strategy loses money.** On the remaining 1,024 closed positions: gross −0.11 SOL, about −0.75 SOL after modelled fees and the 0.0003 SOL tips, i.e. **−0.00073 SOL per trade (−7.3% of each 0.01 SOL position)**, with a position-level win rate of 19%.
   - 335 of them (33%) never received a single price update after entry and were closed by the 45 s stagnant timeout at the entry price, paying round-trip fees for nothing.
   - 875 of the 1,024 first exits were stagnant timeouts; only 29 reached take-profit tier 1 honestly.
   - Costs dominate: two 0.0003 SOL tips are 6% of a 0.01 SOL position before slippage. Paper also fills at the create-event price about 1.1 s (screening p50) before a live buy could land, and assumes the full rent refund, so live results would be worse still.
3. **The 97% win rate is a reporting artefact (BUG-105).** `pumpfun-audit pnl` scores each sell on its own, so the 0.0015 SOL rent refunded on a full exit makes a flat stagnant exit look like a +0.00096 SOL win, while the rent paid sits on the buy row. Win rate and expectancy must be computed per position (buy + all its sells).
4. **The parity check "passes" but proves nothing yet.** Replay and live agree within tolerance (1,098 entries in both, 986 with the same exit, 66 live-only; net 65.65 vs 60.76 SOL, allowed ±6.56) only because both read the same wrong prices. Re-run it after the BUG-104 fix.
5. **Operationally the run is healthy so far:** no exceptions, no stream drops, stable-looking memory, and the stream, screening, paper execution and exit stack all ran for 14 h.

### Decision
- **Stage 0 cannot pass on this build.** BUG-104 makes paper PnL meaningless, and the corrected figures are negative. Do not start Stage 1.
- Keep the pilot running to 72 h for the operational criteria (uptime, memory, stream stability). Its PnL is only useful after correcting for BUG-104.
- Before the next Stage 0: fix BUG-104 (reject or correctly price non-standard curves, and price paper entries from the curve), BUG-105 (per-position win rate) and BUG-107 (holder data), configure an alert provider, then re-tag and restart Stage 0 with a fresh `data/`.
- Tuning ([`RUNBOOK.md` §6.1](../RUNBOOK.md#61-replay-parity-and-tuning-on-captures-sprint-24c)) waits for a clean capture: the main levers are the stagnant timeout (45 s closes a third of positions with no data), the tip size relative to 0.01 SOL positions, and entry selection.
